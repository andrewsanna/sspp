What Sunday morning looks like

The shape of the morning from start to finish, and a few things you might notice along the way.

1

Arrive

Come anytime between 8:15 and 9:30. Parking is free in the lot, and someone near the door will be glad to point you in.

2

Divine Liturgy

Begins at 9:30 AM. Stand, sit, or simply watch. Nobody expects you to know the service.

3

Coffee hour

Join us downstairs for coffee after the Liturgy. It is the easiest place to say hello.

A few things you might notice
Icons

Images of Christ and the Saints line the walls, reminding us of their unseen presence in the house of worship.

Incense

A sweet, smoky scent fills the church during parts of the service. It is part of how we pray.

Candles

Many light a candle in the Narthex, remembering Christ's words: "I am the light of the world." Feel free to light one, no instructions needed.

Sign of the cross

You'll see people cross themselves at different moments to bless and protect themselves. It's personal, and easy to simply observe.

Frequently asked questions

If your question isn't here, ask us. We would rather you ask than wonder.

Can I visit if I'm not Orthodox?
Yes. Our doors are open to everyone, and no one is turned away. Many of our parishioners became Orthodox Christians as adults, from many different backgrounds. Someone from our welcome team is always glad to meet you when you visit.
Can I bring my kids?
Please do. Children are part of our worship, and there is no separate children's service. If you need a quieter moment, the nursery is behind the narthex, and a parent and child room is at the bottom of the stairs.
How will I know what to do?
You won't be expected to know anything. Stand, sit, and follow along as much as you can, and ask someone nearby if you need help.
What should I wear?
Come as you are comfortable. Most parishioners dress smart-casual: slacks, skirts, blazers. Please avoid shorts, jeans, tank tops, sandals, and hats.
Can I receive communion?
The Holy Eucharist is offered only to baptized and chrismated Orthodox Christians who are properly prepared. Non-Orthodox visitors are invited to come forward during that part of the service to receive a blessing from the priest and a small piece of blessed bread (antidoron). Please come forward with your arms crossed over your shoulders and say your name.
How long is the service?
Sunday Divine Liturgy is approximately 1.5 hours, not counting special services like memorials, and can run slightly longer during busier times of the year. You're welcome to arrive partway through if that fits your morning better.
