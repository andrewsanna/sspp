Your first visit

What Sunday morning looks like

Here is the shape of the morning, start to finish.

1

Arrive

Come anytime between 8:15 and 9:30. Parking is free in the lot, and someone near the door will be glad to point you in.

2

Orthros

Morning prayers begin at 8:15. Feel free to arrive partway through — many parishioners do.

3

Divine Liturgy

Our main service begins at 9:30. Stand, sit, or simply take it in — there is no expectation either way.

4

Social Hour

After the service, join us downstairs for coffee and conversation. This is the easiest place to say hello.

What you'll see

A few things you might notice

Orthodox worship engages all the senses — come ready to be present and take part.

Icons

Images of Christ and the Saints line the walls, signifying and reminding us of their unseen presence in the house of worship.

Incense

A sweet, smoky scent fills the church during parts of the service. It is part of how we worship, not a distraction from it.

Candles

Many people light a candle in the Narthex near the entrance, saying a quiet prayer and remembering Christ's words: 'I am the light of the world.' Feel free to light one, no instructions needed.

Sign of the Cross

You will see people cross themselves at different moments to bless and protect themselves. It is personal, and easy to simply observe.

Frequently Asked Questions

If your question is not here, reach out — we would rather you ask than wonder.

Can I visit if I'm not Orthodox?
"Absolutely! Our doors are open to everyone, and no one is turned away. Many of our parishioners became Orthodox Christians as adults, from many different backgrounds. Someone from our welcome team is always glad to meet you when you visit.
Can I bring my kids?
Please do. Children are part of our worship, not separate from it — there is no children's service to step out for. If you need a quieter moment, our nursery is located behind the narthex, and a parent/children room is available at the bottom of the stairs./div>
How will I know what to do?
You won't be expected to know anything. Stand, sit, and follow along as much as you're able — and please don't hesitate to ask someone nearby if you need guidance.
What should I wear?
Come as you are comfortable. Most parishioners dress in smart-casual attire — slacks, skirts, blazers. We ask that you avoid shorts, jeans, tank tops, sandals, and hats.
Can I receive communion?
The Holy Eucharist is offered only to baptized and chrismated Orthodox Christians who are properly prepared. Non-Orthodox visitors are warmly invited to come forward during that part of the service to receive a blessing from the priest and a small piece of blessed bread (antidoron). Please come forward with your arms crossed over your shoulders and say your name.
How long is the service?
Sunday Divine Liturgy is approximately 1.5 hours, not counting special services like memorials, and can run slightly longer during busier times of the year. You're welcome to arrive partway through if that fits your morning better.
