Baptism

Welcoming a new member into the faith

Baptism is how a child (or an adult who wasn't baptized in infancy) is joined to Christ and the Church. Done by immersion in water and followed by Chrismation (receiving the Holy Spirit), it is often followed by a celebration with family and friends.

  • Godparents (sponsors): at least one godparent is required and must be a baptized, practicing Orthodox Christian in good standing with the Church.
  • Scheduling: baptisms typically occur on Saturdays or after Sunday Liturgy. Parents must meet with the priest to plan at least two months in advance.
  • What's needed: a list of items needed will be provided upon request along with several documents to guide the preparations.
  • Adult baptism: adults coming to the faith for the first time typically go through a a year long period of instruction and preparation (Catechumenate) that includes a catechism course, frequent attendance at worship services and individual guidance with the priest.

Chrismation

Received into the Orthodox faith

Chrismation is the Sacrament by which someone who is already baptized in another Christian Trinitarian faith, is received into the Orthodox Church through the oil of Chrism bestowing the gift of the Holy Spirit.

  • Preparation: those preparing for Chrismation is the Sacrament by which someone who is already baptized in another Christian Trinitarian faith, is received into the Orthodox Church through the oil of Chrism bestowing the gift of the Holy Spirit.
  • Sponsor: a sponsor who is a practicing Orthodox Christian in good standing is required, similar to a godparent at baptism.
  • Timing: Catechumens are received into the Orthodox Church, through the Sacrament of Chrismation during a Sunday Divine Liturgy on or near a major feast day. Thus, the person is welcomed into the Church by the whole parish family.

Wedding

Beginning your marriage in the Church

The Sacrament of Marriage unites a couple as husband and wife with the blessing of Jesus Christ. It's one of the most joyful services in the Orthodox Church — full of ancient symbolism, from the rings, to the crowns to the shared cup.

  • Scheduling: please reach out to the parish office at least 1 year before your intended date, and before booking a reception venue — some dates are restricted by the Church calendar including major feast days and fast periods. You will be required to meet with the priest to discuss eligibility and begin planning.
  • Interfaith couples: we regularly celebrate weddings where one spouse is a baptized Christian, not Orthodox. They are not required to convert.
  • Koumbaro/Koumbara (sponsor): at least one sponsor must be a baptized Orthodox Christian in good standing with his/her local Greek Orthodox Church.
  • Pre-marital counseling: couples go through a structured comprehensive program meeting with the priest monthly over a period of one year.
Start planning a wedding: Email Fr. Rick

Confession

The Sacrament of Forgiveness, Reconciliation, Healing and Renewal

Confession is a private sacramental service with the priest — an opportunity to be honest and open before God about where you've missed the mark, and to receive His forgiveness and guidance.

  • Scheduling: confession is by appointment with Fr. Rick or Fr. Peter — just reach out directly or through the parish office to find a time.
  • New to confession? that's completely alright to say up front. Many people preparing for Chrismation or returning to the Church after time away go to confession for the first time as adults.
  • Confidentiality: what's shared in confession is held in strict confidence.
