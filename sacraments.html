Birth, Naming & Churching

Welcoming a new life

The Church honors the birth of a child with prayers for mother and baby soon after birth — whether in the hospital or at home — followed by the naming of the child on the eighth day, and a 40-Day Churching service in the sanctuary.

  • Right after birth: please contact the priest soon after your baby is born to arrange an initial visit and prayer.
  • 40-Day Churching: contact the parish office to schedule — this can be done on a Sunday or a weekday, whenever works for your family.

Baptism

Welcoming a new member into the faith

Baptism is how a child (or an adult who wasn't baptized in infancy) is joined to Christ and the Church. Done by immersion in water and followed by Chrismation (receiving the Holy Spirit), it is often followed by a celebration with family and friends.

  • Godparents (sponsors): at least one godparent is required and must be a baptized, practicing Orthodox Christian in good standing with the Church.
  • Scheduling: baptisms typically occur on Saturdays or after Sunday Liturgy. Parents must meet with the priest to plan at least two months in advance.
  • What's needed: a list of items needed will be provided upon request along with several documents to guide the preparations.
  • Adult baptism: adults coming to the faith for the first time typically go through a year-long period of instruction and preparation (Catechumenate) that includes a catechism course, frequent attendance at worship services, and individual guidance with the priest.

Chrismation

Received into the Orthodox faith

Chrismation is the Sacrament by which someone who is already baptized in another Christian Trinitarian faith is received into the Orthodox Church through the oil of Chrism, bestowing the gift of the Holy Spirit.

  • Preparation: those preparing for Chrismation typically go through a year-long period of instruction (Catechumenate) that includes a catechism course, frequent attendance at worship services, and individual guidance with the priest.
  • Sponsor: a sponsor who is a practicing Orthodox Christian in good standing is required, similar to a godparent at baptism.
  • Timing: Catechumens are received into the Orthodox Church through the Sacrament of Chrismation during a Sunday Divine Liturgy, on or near a major feast day — welcomed into the Church by the whole parish family.
Ask about Chrismation: Email Fr. Rick
Confession

The Sacrament of Forgiveness, Reconciliation, Healing and Renewal

Confession is a private sacramental service with the priest — an opportunity to be honest and open before God about where you've missed the mark, and to receive His forgiveness and guidance.

  • Preparing: this short guide walks through the Ten Commandments and Christ's Beatitudes as a way to reflect beforehand — come ready to talk about what's weighing on you most.
  • When to come: Confession is available anytime, and many find fasting seasons like Great Lent, the Nativity Fast (Nov. 15–Dec. 24), or the Dormition Fast (Aug. 1–15) a natural time to go, since fasting and repentance go hand in hand. Most parishioners come at least once a year — there's no set schedule, come as often as it helps.
  • Scheduling: Confession is by appointment with Fr. Rick or Fr. Peter — just reach out directly or through the parish office to find a time.
  • New to confession? That's completely alright to say up front. Many people preparing for Chrismation or returning to the Church after time away go to confession for the first time as adults.
  • Confidentiality: what's shared in confession is held in strict confidence.

Holy Unction

Prayer and anointing for healing

Holy Unction is the Sacrament of anointing with blessed oil for healing of body and soul — offered for those who are sick, facing surgery, or simply in need of God's strength and comfort.

  • Scheduling: please contact the parish office or Fr. Rick directly to arrange a time — either at the church or, when needed, at home or in the hospital.
Ask about Holy Unction: Email Fr. Rick
Wedding

Beginning your marriage in the Church

The Sacrament of Marriage unites a couple as husband and wife with the blessing of Jesus Christ. It's one of the most joyful services in the Orthodox Church — full of ancient symbolism, from the rings, to the crowns, to the shared cup.

  • Scheduling: please reach out to the parish office at least 1 year before your intended date, and before booking a reception venue — some dates are restricted by the Church calendar, including major feast days and fast periods. You will be required to meet with the priest to discuss eligibility and begin planning.
  • Interfaith couples: we regularly celebrate weddings where one spouse is a baptized Christian, not Orthodox. That spouse is not required to convert.
  • Koumbaro/Koumbara (sponsor): at least one sponsor must be a baptized Orthodox Christian in good standing with his/her local Greek Orthodox Church.
  • Pre-marital counseling: couples go through a structured program, meeting with the priest monthly over a period of one year.

When a marriage ends: marriages can go through real strain, and we'd rather walk with you through it than just at the end — please reach out early if you're facing serious difficulty, as counseling can make a real difference. If a marriage has ended in permanent separation and civil divorce, the Church also asks that an ecclesiastical divorce be obtained before either spouse can be married again in the Church. Contact Fr. Rick to talk through next steps.

Memorial

Remembering our loved ones

A brief memorial service can be scheduled on Sundays or other days through the parish office — traditionally offered at 40 days, 3 months, 6 months, 9 months, one year, and each anniversary after.

  • Kolyva: the boiled wheat dish shared at memorials is an important part of the tradition. It can be made at home (recipe here) or ordered through a local vendor.
