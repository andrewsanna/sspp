Sacred Milestones in the Life of Faith

From baptisms and weddings to memorial services, the sacraments are where God meets us in the defining moments of life. Here is what to expect for each service and how our priests can help you prepare.

Birth & Churching Baptism Chrismation Confession Unction Marriage Memorials Funeral

Σαραντισμός

Birth, Naming & Churching

The Church marks a child's birth with prayers for mother and baby soon after the birth, in the hospital or at home. The child is named on the eighth day, and at 40 days mother and child come to church for the Churching service.

Right after birth
Contact Fr. Rick to arrange a visit and prayers.
40-Day Churching
Contact the parish office to schedule. It can be on a Sunday or a weekday, whichever works for your family.

How to arrange: Email Fr. Rick after the birth, or call the parish office to schedule the 40-day Churching.

Βάπτισμα

Baptism

Baptism joins a child, or an adult who was not baptized as an infant, to Christ and the Church. It is done by immersion in water and followed right away by Chrismation, the gift of the Holy Spirit. Families often celebrate afterward with relatives and friends.

Godparents
At least one godparent is required and must be a baptized, practicing Orthodox Christian in good standing with the Church.
Scheduling
Baptisms usually take place on Saturdays or after Sunday Liturgy. Parents meet with the priest to plan at least two months in advance.
What's needed
We'll give you a list of the items you'll need, along with documents to guide your preparations.
Adult baptism
Adults coming to the faith for the first time usually spend about a year in instruction and preparation (the Catechumenate): a catechism course, regular attendance at services, and individual guidance from the priest.

How to arrange: Email Fr. Rick at least two months before the date you have in mind.

Email Fr. Rick (847) 729-2235

Χρίσμα

Chrismation

Chrismation receives someone already baptized in another Trinitarian Christian church into the Orthodox Church. They are anointed with the oil of Chrism, which gives the gift of the Holy Spirit.

Preparation
About a year of instruction (the Catechumenate), including a catechism course, regular attendance at services, and individual guidance from the priest.
Sponsor
A practicing Orthodox Christian in good standing, as a godparent is at baptism.
Timing
Catechumens are received during a Sunday Divine Liturgy on or near a major feast day, in front of the whole parish.

How to arrange: Email Fr. Rick to ask about the catechism class and next steps.

Email Fr. Rick (847) 729-2235

Εξομολόγηση

Confession

Confession is a private sacrament with the priest: a chance to be honest before God about where you have fallen short, and to receive His forgiveness and guidance.

Preparing
This short guide walks through the Ten Commandments and the Beatitudes as a way to reflect beforehand.
When to come
Any time. Many people go during a fasting season, such as Great Lent, the Nativity Fast (Nov. 15 to Dec. 24), or the Dormition Fast (Aug. 1 to 15). Most parishioners come at least once a year.
Scheduling
By appointment with Fr. Rick or Fr. Peter.
First time?
It's fine to say so at the start. Many people preparing for Chrismation, or returning after time away, make their first confession as adults.
Confidentiality
What's said in confession is held in strict confidence.

How to arrange: Contact Fr. Rick or Fr. Peter directly, or call the parish office to find a time.

Email Fr. Rick (847) 729-2235

Ευχέλαιο

Holy Unction

Holy Unction is anointing with blessed oil for the healing of body and soul. It is offered to anyone who is sick, facing surgery, or in need of God's strength and comfort.

Where
At the church or, when needed, at home or in the hospital.

How to arrange: Call the parish office or email Fr. Rick to arrange a time.

Email Fr. Rick (847) 729-2235

Γάμος

Marriage

The Sacrament of Marriage unites a couple as husband and wife with the blessing of Jesus Christ. It is one of the most joyful services in the Orthodox Church, with the exchange of rings, the crowning, and the common cup.

Scheduling
Contact the parish office at least one year before your intended date, and before you book a reception venue. Some dates are restricted by the Church calendar, including major feast days and fast periods.
Interfaith couples
We regularly celebrate weddings where one spouse is a baptized Christian who is not Orthodox. That spouse is not required to convert.
Koumbaro / Koumbara
At least one sponsor must be a baptized Orthodox Christian in good standing with their local Greek Orthodox parish.
Pre-marital counseling
Couples meet with the priest monthly for a year in a structured program.
When a marriage is struggling
Reach out early; counseling can make a real difference. If a marriage has ended in civil divorce, the Church asks for an ecclesiastical divorce before either spouse marries again in the Church.

How to arrange: Call the parish office at least a year ahead of your date.

Email Fr. Rick (847) 729-2235

Μνημόσυνο

Memorials

A memorial service can be held on a Sunday or another day. Memorials are traditionally offered at 40 days, 3 months, 6 months, 9 months, and one year after a death, and on each anniversary after that.

Kolyva
The boiled wheat shared at memorials. You can make it at home (recipe) or order it from a local vendor.

How to arrange: Call the parish office to schedule a memorial.

(847) 729-2235

Κηδεία

Funeral

Funerals are best planned well ahead of time, with a funeral home and the parish. When a death is sudden, the parish will help you through each step.

If a loved one has just died
Call the parish office right away, any day of the week. Dial 2 for after-hours emergencies. We work closely with the funeral home and will guide you through planning the service.
Traditions
Read our guidelines on Orthodox Christian funeral traditions.

How to arrange: Call the parish office at any hour.
